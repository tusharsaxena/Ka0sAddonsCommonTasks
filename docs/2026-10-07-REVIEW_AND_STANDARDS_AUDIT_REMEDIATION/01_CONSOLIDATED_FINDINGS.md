## Method

Each cluster went through three passes:

1. **Reality skeptic.** Re-read the cited code and docs on the feature branch and tried to refute the finding. Outcome: `confirmed` (reproduces as written), `corrected` (real, but the location, severity, cause or remediation was wrong and has been fixed), or `refuted` (not a defect).
2. **Necessity skeptic.** Assumed the finding is real and argued that it does not need fixing: is the rule actually binding, does anyone hit it, is the cost worth it, does a pending upstream change make it moot, is the "fix" churn that goes stale on the next commit.
3. **Reconciler.** Weighed both and set the final `needs_addressing`, `severity_after`, `fix_home` and remediation, plus a cluster summary.

`needs_addressing` grades:

- **must**: a player-visible bug, data loss or wrong output, or a tooling defect that blocks or misdirects every future run. Fix in this exercise.
- **should**: a real breach of a MUST rule or a real defect with low impact. Fix in this exercise unless the owner says otherwise.
- **optional**: real but cosmetic, latent, or SHOULD-level; fold in when the file is already open.
- **no**: refuted, or real but not worth changing (sanctioned by the standard, documented by design, or churn). Listed in one place below so the owner can overrule.

Source bundles: `<Repo>/docs/audits/2026-10-07/` and `<Repo>/docs/reviews/2026-10-07/`. Raw findings: `inputs/findings/<Repo>.json`. Clusters: `inputs/clusters.json`. Verdicts: `inputs/verdicts/<Cnn>.json`. Merged machine-readable copy: `inputs/verified_findings.json`.
